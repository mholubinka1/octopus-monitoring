# Incomplete recent days fall back to standing-charge-only, not full exclusion

Octopus's consumption API has a real settlement lag (observed directly: a day can sit at 2/48 or 0/48 half-hourly rows more than 24 hours after it ended, then fill in over the following day or two). Any query that groups by calendar day and treats "yesterday" or a billing period's elapsed days as final was silently showing a misleadingly low total for a day still arriving, rather than genuinely reflecting the day's incomplete row count. The fix is a completeness guard (`COUNT(*) = 48`), applied only to *strictly past* days — never to the current in-progress day, which is expected to be partial by definition.

For `CostForecastRetriever`'s billing-period accounting, an incomplete past day is treated exactly like the existing zero-consumption gap case: it disappears from `read_elapsed_billing_period_costs`'s results and is picked up by `_fill_zero_consumption_days`, contributing only its standing charge to `actual_cost_to_date` until it settles and reappears complete on a later refresh. We considered excluding it entirely (no standing charge either, counted as a future/remaining day instead) — that's more "correct" in principle, but it means the same day's standing charge silently jumps from `remaining_days` to `daily_costs` between refreshes depending on settlement timing, adding accounting complexity for a rare, self-correcting, bounded-magnitude gap. The standing-charge floor was chosen deliberately as the simpler, already-existing mechanism; don't "fix" it into full exclusion without revisiting this trade-off.

Filled days (both genuine zero-consumption and lag-incomplete) are excluded from `project_daily_average_consumption`'s input — they don't represent real observed usage, and letting them count as zero-kWh days would drag the future-cost projection down every time a recent day hasn't fully arrived, which per the observed lag pattern is common, not rare.
